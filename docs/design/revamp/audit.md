## 1. Executive summary

The product's foundations are sound (tenancy-aware shell, accessible form primitives, careful copy, complete Telugu, an honest privacy model), and the automated accessibility baseline is clean. What makes CampusLink feel slow and un-premium is how screens are loaded and stitched together, not any single screen. The ten findings that matter most:

1. **Every first visit to a screen goes spinner → skeleton → content, and the data only starts after the code.** Routes are lazy components with no loaders or prefetch, so each tap waits for the code chunk, renders a skeleton, then fetches. When the old and new screens have different wrappers in `router.tsx`, React mounts a new Suspense boundary and shows a full-height spinner for at least 300 ms, even on a fast connection. On a throttled phone this is 0.65–0.85 s per first visit (§5, TR-1, TR-2).
2. **A screen that can't load its code crashes the whole app.** Offline, or after a deploy replaces chunk files, opening any unvisited screen replaces the shell with React Router's raw "Unexpected Application Error! Failed to fetch dynamically imported module…" page. There are no error boundaries anywhere (ST-0).
3. **Scroll position is never managed.** Back from a post lands Home at 222 px instead of 900 px; back to Jobs lands 500 px off; a tab round-trip resets to the top; a new tab can open at the previous tab's offset; tapping the active tab does nothing (TR-3, NB-3).
4. **Navigation is the same for every role and hides each role's real work.** The bottom bar is capped at four student tabs, so Messages and Sessions (students), Requests and Mentoring (alumni) and Admin (staff) sit in a 15–16 item More sheet. On desktop the rail (18 items for a student, up to 21 for an admin) doesn't scroll: at 1440 × 900 a student cannot reach Notifications, Profile, Settings, Search or Sign out (IA-1, IA-2).
5. **The first load is too heavy and too sequential.** 301 KB of gzipped JS before anything renders (budget 170 KB: both languages, every Zod schema, sockets and form libraries are on the critical path), followed by five serial network steps. Signed-in first visits score 54–70 on Lighthouse mobile, with LCP 4.1–4.9 s against a 2.5 s budget (PF-1, §4.11).
6. **There are no transitions or celebrations.** Screens hard-cut, sheets pop in, and accepting a mentee, booking a session, finishing a roadmap step or earning a badge pass with a toast at best. Beyond the landing mesh, the mentor swipe card, one progress bar and press feedback on buttons, the brief's motion principles are not applied (MO-1, MO-2, TR-4).
7. **Layouts break at 360 px.** Job detail and the alumni Mentoring dashboard are wider than the screen, which pushes the notification bell to or past the screen edge. The profile header crushes the member's name into one word per line. The translucent bars let body text run through their labels (A11Y-4, TY-1, NB-2).
8. **Feedback is thin.** 4 of 99 actions are optimistic. About 6 of 105 data views can show an error; the rest show "nothing here" when a request fails, including offline. Keyboard focus falls to the page body after Send, Save and Accept (ST-2, ST-4, A11Y-6).
9. **Some content undermines credibility.** The public landing page shows ten fictional "verified" alumni to every college in production. Copy still promises features that shipped phases ago ("verification arrives next", "when mentorship opens", "Coming next: Career Galaxy"). Platform admins land on "We couldn't find that page" after every sign-in (VI-1, MC-1, IA-10).
10. **Members can be signed out silently.** Refresh tokens rotate with no tolerance for a lost response, so a backgrounded app or a dropped connection during refresh revokes the session on the next launch. This was observed during the audit. It is a security trade-off and needs your decision (PW-8).

The fastest visible improvement is in §8: about two dozen fixes, each under half a day. The structural fixes (loaders + prefetch + a stable Suspense boundary, scroll restoration, role-aware navigation, and history-aware sheets) are grouped as revamp phase R1 in §9.

## 4. Detailed findings

Severity: **Critical** blocks the "seamless, premium" goal on most sessions or loses work/state; **High** is visible on a main path for many members; **Medium** is noticeable or affects a subset; **Low** is polish. Effort: **S** under a day, **M** a few days, **L** a week or more. Screenshot names refer to `before/`; `data/` files hold the raw measurements.

### 4.1 Visual identity — score 3/5

The "Night campus" direction is real and distinctive on the public pages (mesh, Bricolage Grotesque, gradient headline). Inside the app it thins out into flat bordered cards on a flat background, and tenant branding only recolours accents. Raw colour discipline is good: outside `tokens.css` and the contrast helpers, hard-coded colours are limited to the avatar gradient, three toggle thumbs, platform defaults and the deliberately photographic story viewer (`data/inventory.json › rawColour`).

## 5. Navigation seamlessness: deep dive

### 5.1 Method

- **Build:** production (`vite build` + `vite preview`, production CSP), seeded demo data, student `ananya@dpit.example` (DPIT) on a 360 × 780 phone viewport at DPR 2, dark theme.
- **Throttling:** Chrome DevTools Protocol, 4× CPU slowdown, 150 ms latency, 1.6 Mbps down / 750 kbps up (Lighthouse's mobile profile). An unthrottled pass is in `data/journeys-unthrottled.json` for comparison.
- **Per animation frame**, an injected probe recorded: the URL, whether the route-fallback spinner was on screen, how many skeletons were in `main`, whether an empty state was shown, how much text `main` held (fewer than 20 characters = "blank"), the identity of the `<header>`, bottom `<nav>` and `<main>` nodes (to detect remounts), `scrollY` and the focused element.
- **Network:** every script and API request with start and end time, to see whether data waited for code.
- **Frames:** CDP screencast frames with timestamps; contact sheets per step are in `frames-sheets/` (one tile per changed frame, labelled "+N ms" from the tap) and the same frames play at real speed in `clips/`. A Playwright trace of the same journeys was also recorded (18 MB, not committed; see §10 Q14). Playwright's own video recording failed in this container (ffmpeg build mismatch), so the clips stand in for video.
- **"Tap → content"** is the time from the tap to the first frame where a selector unique to the destination's _loaded_ state is visible (e.g. the first job card, the mentor's session-type picker), with no route spinner on screen.
- The fresh-database runs make every step reproducible; numbers vary by ±15% between runs on this machine (two throttled runs compared).

Frame counts are at ~60 fps, so 19 spinner frames ≈ 0.3 s of spinner.

### 5.3 What happens, frame by frame

**J0 · Cold start (reload Home): 2.9 s to the first post.** Frames: nothing new is painted for 0.88 s; 0.88–1.51 s a spinner on an otherwise empty screen (no top bar, no bottom bar); at 1.59 s the shell paints with a **second** spinner filling the content area until 2.28 s; 2.36–2.58 s skeletons; 2.64 s One Useful Thing; 2.89 s the first feed post. The request log shows why it is a strict chain: initial JS (11 files) 255–596 ms → `/tenant` 866–1,041 ms → `/auth/refresh` 1,058–1,225 ms → `/me` 1,273–1,433 ms → the Home chunk and its 18 dependencies 1,568–2,144 ms → six Home queries 2,333–2,624 ms. Only `/me` truly depends on the step before it. _(sheet: `frames-sheets/00-cold--reload-home.png`, clip: `clips/00-cold--reload-home.webp`)_

**J1 · Home → post → back.** Tapping the comment icon at scroll 900 px: the post-page chunk request only starts **275 ms after the tap** (React must first render the lazy element inside the transition), and for ≈0.3 s the whole content area is a viewport-tall spinner (first visit to `/posts/:id` crosses from a `<S>` route to a `<RequirePermission><S>` route, see 5.4). Then a serial chain: chunk 275–437 ms → `/posts/:id` 628–804 ms → `/posts/:id/comments` 845–1,012 ms; the post was already in the feed cache. Back: Home re-renders from cache in ~0.55 s but **lands at 222 px instead of 900 px**: the browser's native scroll restoration runs before the virtualised feed has re-measured, then is never corrected.

**J2 · Home → Mentors → Sneha → book → confirmation.** First visit to Mentors: ≈0.3 s of full-height spinner (+12 skeleton frames) while `motion` (43 KB) and the Mentors chunk load. Browse: 10 skeleton frames (new query key). Opening Sneha: **no spinner** (Mentors → mentor page keep the same wrapper shape) but also **no feedback for ≈0.4 s**: the Mentors list stays on screen, un-dimmed, while the mentor-page chunk loads (254–430 ms), then 12 frames of skeleton while `/mentors/:id` loads (516–676 ms), although the tile the student tapped already held the name, headline, company and topics. After _Send request_, the form is replaced by the confirmation card: the page shrinks, **scroll jumps from 1,032 px to 23 px**, and **keyboard focus falls to `<body>`** because the submit button is disabled while loading and then removed. There is no motion, no calendar action and no "what happens next" timeline.

**J3 · Tab switching.** Round 1 (first visits): Jobs shows ≈0.3 s of spinner (178–494 ms), then skeletons that start **at Home's old scroll offset** (the Job Radar intro is cut off at the top) and whose layout doesn't match the result (a list skeleton, then a "For you" carousel appears above it), and finally jumps to the top at ≈1.2 s (764 ms to the first card; `frames-sheets/03-tabs--r0-jobs.png`); Roadmaps leaves the _previous_ screen frozen for 0.47 s, **including the old tab highlight** (the tapped tab only lights up when the new screen commits), then shows skeletons, then "My roadmaps" pops in at 0.73 s and pushes the filters down (the layout shift Lighthouse scores at 0.189; `frames-sheets/03-tabs--r0-roadmaps.png`). The difference comes only from the wrapper shapes in `router.tsx`. Round 2 (all chunks and data cached) still takes 0.2–0.55 s per tab under 4× CPU, because each tab remounts from scratch (Home: stories, One Useful Thing, the virtualised feed) and fires its queries again (`staleTime` 0). **Scroll is never kept per tab:** Home at 1,200 px → Jobs → Home lands at 0 (`data/scroll-memory.json`). **Tapping the active tab does nothing** (stays at 800 px). Rapid fire (8 taps, 250 ms apart) produced **18 API calls**, none cancelled, and the final screen settled 1.2 s after the last tap. The shell (`<header>`, bottom `<nav>`, `<main>`) never remounted on any client-side navigation (good).

**J4 · Jobs (scrolled to 1,400 px) → job → back.** No spinner (Jobs → job keep the same wrapper), the list stays frozen for ≈0.1 s, then 22 skeleton frames. **J5 · Roadmaps → roadmap → step → back gesture.** Roadmap page: list frozen, then 15 skeleton frames (758 ms). **J6 · Notifications → item (as Sneha, mentor).** Notifications: 13 skeleton frames (810 ms). **J7 · Profile → edit → save.** Profile is reached through More (2 taps). 14 spinner frames + 11 skeleton frames (826 ms). Save shows a toast after 0.34 s; focus falls to `<body>` (the Save button disables while saving).

**J9 · Browser back and forward.** Fast (57–152 ms) because chunks and data are cached; no transition, no scroll restoration beyond what the browser does natively.

## 7. Per-role narratives

Each narrative follows the demo account through the README's "Try" flows on a 360 px phone (dark and light) and at 1440 px. Every role, including staff and the college admin, inherits the student permission set (`packages/shared/src/roles.ts:85-145`), so **every signed-in role gets the same four bottom tabs: Home, Jobs, Roadmaps, Mentors**, plus More (`app-shell.tsx:101-175`). That single decision shapes most of what follows.

### Student (Ananya, `ananya@dpit.example`)

- **Delights.** The landing page and sign-in are the most polished screens in the product: the mesh, the type and the copy set a confident tone (`public-landing-360-dark-dpit.png`). Alumni Match explains _why_ a mentor fits ("Same branch (CSE)", "Shares your skills: java"), which is more honest than Topmate's ranking. Job detail asks "Did you apply?" when you come back from the employer. The Job Radar's "1 alumnus can refer" chip is exactly the LinkedIn-credible signal the product promises. Telugu is complete (1,794 of 1,794 keys).
- **Frustrates.** Her two most time-sensitive destinations, **Sessions** (her mock interview starts in five minutes) and **Messages** (Rahul's reply), are not in the bottom bar; they sit first and second in a 15-item More sheet, and Home on a phone never shows "Next session" (it lives in the right rail, `hidden xl:block`, `home.tsx:320`). Joining the call is four taps from Home (More → Sessions → Join video → Open). Every first visit to a tab flashes a full-height spinner and then skeletons; a tab round-trip back to Home starts at the top, and back from a post lands somewhere else. The glass bottom bar shows the feed text sliding underneath its labels. Requesting Sneha means filling three free-text boxes before a time can be sent, and the success state is a card that silently replaces the form.
- **Missing.** A "today" surface (next session, unread replies, pending request status) above the feed; tab memory; pull to refresh; a celebration when a mentor accepts or a roadmap step is done (her streak and XP exist, but nothing marks the moment); deep links that survive sign-in.

### Alumni (Rahul, `rahul@dpit.example`)

## 10. Open questions (your decision)

1. **Role-aware navigation.** May the bottom bar differ by role (proposed in IA-1), or must every member see the same tabs?
2. **The landing "alumni wall".** Remove it, or build an opt-in flow so each college can show real alumni who consented?
3. **Session robustness vs. strict reuse detection.** Is a short grace window (for example 10 s, the previous token returning the same successor) acceptable for the refresh rotation, or should we keep strict revocation and coordinate refresh across tabs only? (PW-8; security first per CLAUDE.md, so this needs an explicit yes.)
4. **Data-loading architecture.** OK to move every screen to React Router loaders + `ensureQueryData` (one pattern for the whole app), rather than adding prefetch around the current component fetching?
5. **Keep-alive tabs.** Instagram-style tabs keep screens mounted, which costs memory on low-end Android phones. Keep tabs alive, or only keep their data and scroll warm?
6. **Bundle budget.** Is 170 KB gzipped still the target? It implies loading Telugu strings only when chosen and moving form and socket libraries off the first load.
7. **College identity in the installed app.** Should each college get its own app name, icon and theme colour (per-tenant manifest + logo upload)? This needs a file purpose and moderation for logos.
8. **Celebrations.** How far should moments go: visual only, or also haptics on Android? Any moments that must stay quiet (for example rejected or expired requests)?
9. **Media delivery.** Signed short-lived URLs or a cookie-scoped file route? This decides whether images and reels can use the HTTP cache and streaming.
10. **Desktop layout.** The brief's three columns (rail, content, right rail) apply only to Home today. Extend the right rail to Jobs, Mentors and Roadmaps? Two-pane messaging?
11. **Default theme.** The brief says dark-first, but the code follows the device theme for "system". Which should the first paint use?
12. **Mentor request structure.** Are the three required text boxes a deliberate quality gate, or can it become one optional box plus prompt chips?
13. **Career Galaxy.** Invest in it as the signature visual, or make the list the primary view and the galaxy a secondary mode?
14. **Evidence retention.** The 18 MB Playwright trace was not committed. Keep traces in the repository (Git LFS), elsewhere, or regenerate on demand?

## Appendix A. Method, coverage and limits

**Environment.** A cloud container: Node 22.22, MongoDB 8.0.34 and Redis 7.4.11 in Docker (pulled through `mirror.gcr.io` because Docker Hub rate-limited), Mailpit, the API under `tsx` with `RATE_LIMIT_SCALE=50` and `INGESTION_ENABLED=false`. The web app was audited as the **production build** served by `vite preview` (the same server and CSP the e2e suite uses) rather than `pnpm dev`, so bundles, the service worker and timings are what members get. The database was re-seeded before each journey pass, so steps are reproducible.

**How the evidence was produced.** Temporary Playwright scripts (now deleted, as agreed) did the following:

- signed in as each demo account (TOTP enrolled and answered for admins);
- visited every main screen at 360 × 780 (DPR 2, touch) and 1440 × 900 in both colour schemes on both tenants, ran axe on every 360 px screen in both themes, and recorded the URLs of detail pages;
- ran ten journeys (J0–J9) with a per-frame DOM probe, request timings and CDP screencast under Lighthouse-like throttling (and once unthrottled);
- measured touch targets, type sizes and horizontal overflow on 32 student screens, the desktop rail, scroll memory, Telugu rendering and offline behaviour.
